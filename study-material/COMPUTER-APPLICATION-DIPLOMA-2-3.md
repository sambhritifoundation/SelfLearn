# Diploma chapters 2–3: teaching and review guide

These chapters follow Diploma chapter 1 and retain internal chapter IDs 12 and 13. Each has six bilingual lessons, 18 practice questions, a guided assignment, an independent variation and an eight-question chapter exam. The written exam scores are provisional and require trainer review.

## Word: create a reusable guide and invitations

Before class, check desktop Word includes Mailings. Learners need the earlier Basic Word and file-management skills. Allow two 45–60 minute sessions and further practice as needed. A printer and image are optional; desktop mail merge is a required skill, so record it as pending if the application is unavailable.

Use the fictional Community Learning Day: 12 October 2026, 10 am–12 noon, Training Hall. Sections: Purpose, Programme, Materials, Contact. Contact is the Training desk. The programme has Welcome / Asha at 10:00, Computer practice / Ravi at 10:15, Questions / Neha at 11:30. The two fictional merge recipients are Asha Das and Ravi Sen.

First session: make a master and monthly copy, apply paragraph styles, build the programme table. Second session: use a separate guide copy for page breaks and automatic contents, personalise letters, review and export. Explain that a one-page notice normally does not need a contents page; the guide copy is deliberate longer-document practice.

Trainer checks: open the files rather than accepting a screenshot alone. Check the master was preserved, Heading 1 is actually applied, the renamed contents entry updates, table rows remain aligned, and the two generated letters have different correct names. Compare the final PDF with the source.

Independent task: 9 November 2026, 11 am–1 pm, Room 2; add Help desk and Neha Roy. Expect an updated guide/PDF and three correct invitations, with October work intact.

## Excel: build a budget that changes correctly

Use desktop Excel. Learners need basic typing, saving and selecting cells. Allow two 45–60 minute sessions. No external account, live price research or personal information is needed.

| Row | Item | Quantity | Rate | Base amount | 10% allowance |

| 3 | Pens | 30 | 10 | 300 | 30 |

Use columns A–E: Item, Quantity, Rate, Amount, Allowance. Put the fictional allowance assumption in G2. Teach `=B2*C2`, then `=D2*$G$2`. Base total: 1,410; allowance total: 141; planned total: 1,551; average item-category amount: 352.50; largest item-category amount: 700. These rates are fictional classroom inputs, not taxes or spending recommendations.

Ask the learner to change an input while you watch. At 25 notebooks, D2 must become 875. At 5% allowance with the baseline quantities, E2:E5 must become 35, 15, 12.50, 8. Restore the baseline before moving on.

Keep formulas on Budget. Copy A1:E5 as values to a separate Analysis sheet for sorting, filtering and chart practice; explicitly explain that this snapshot needs refreshing after Budget changes. Greater than 250 shows two records; greater than or equal to 250 shows three. Ordinary SUM includes filtered rows; do not describe it as a visible-only total.

Independent quantities: 25, 40, 12, 5, with original rates and 8% allowance. Expect base amounts 875, 400, 300, 200; base total 1,775; allowance 142; planned total 1,917. The chart should compare only four base amounts, without a fifth grand-total bar.

## Feedback record

For each assignment quality criterion record: needs another attempt / completed with help / completed independently. Name the actual evidence reviewed, one strength and one correction. Give a changed task for the retry. A checked browser box is self-report; files and demonstrated performance are the evidence. Suggested time is not a pass threshold.
